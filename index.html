About this reconstruction

This is a speculative reconstruction. Today's terrain and imagery are real. The ancient volcano follows Balla (1978) and Karátson et al. (2019): a cone about 13 km across and about 1,200 m high, active 15.0–14.3 million years ago. Its gullies, lava flows, domes and the eruption are modelled, and fine detail below the data resolution is generated.

The time column runs from the oldest stage at the bottom to today at the top. Eruption stages use different time scales; the elapsed-time label, dome, particles and deposits share one clock. Pause freezes the event, and scrubbing reconstructs it deterministically; the wavy lines mark where the time scale changes.

The eruption uses a schematic opening notch, an energy-based runout approximation and size-dependent ash settling in an assumed wind. Plume rise follows an entraining buoyant-parcel approximation in an assumed atmosphere. Deposit intensity and the eruption parameters are illustrative, not calibrated hazard estimates.
